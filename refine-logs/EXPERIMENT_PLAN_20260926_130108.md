# Experiment Plan

**Problem:** certified robust-SLNR selection of N of M desired-phase-aligned pinching-antenna sites under per-element actuator-position boxes (one waveguide, desired receiver D, protected receiver P).
**Method Thesis:** For desired-phase-aligned pinching sites, cheap sector enclosures with exact marginal phase and amplitude ranges yield a certified worst-case SLNR. Maximizing it selects sites whose worst-case SLNR certifiably exceeds that of the exhaustive nominal optimum in the same matched family.
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Target:** IEEE ICC 2027, 6 pages, deadline 2026-10-02.
**Status:** PLAN ONLY. Per the user's instruction, no experiment has been run for this plan. The pilot numbers in `FINAL_PROPOSAL.md` are *not* paper evidence.
**Inputs:** `refine-logs/FINAL_PROPOSAL.md`, `REVIEW_SUMMARY.md`, `REFINEMENT_REPORT.md`; external review in `idea-stage/IDEA_REPORT.md` § External Critical Review.

## Experiment Blocks

### Block B0: Sanity and validity (MUST-RUN)

- **Claim tested:** preconditions for C1. The bounds are valid, there is no artificial gap at $\epsilon = 0$, and results are reproducible.
- **Why this block exists:** reviewer concerns about bound validity, the zero-error pad (−2.78% artifact), and provenance.
- **Task:** 20 cases sampled from the declared grid (fixed list saved), plus all $\epsilon = 0$ rows.
- **Compared systems:** none; these are checks.
- **Metrics:**
  - (i) for 10⁵ random feasible δ per case (uniform + corners), no instance of $W_{\rm sample} < L$ or $> U$;
  - (ii) at $\epsilon = 0$: $L = U =$ exact nominal SLNR, so $S_R = S_N$ and certified gain = 0;
  - (iii) angular-grid refinement at $K \in \{720, 1440, 5760\}$ changes $L$ by less than 0.5%;
  - (iv) clearance assert passes for all ε;
  - (v) rerunning the driver reproduces the CSV bit-for-bit.
- **Setup:** `a7_main.py` plus a small `a7_checks.py` (to be written).
- **Success criterion:** all five checks pass.
- **Failure interpretation:** any violation of (i) is a bug in the certificates. Stop and fix before B1.
- **Table / figure target:** one sentence in the paper plus a supplementary note.
- **Priority:** MUST-RUN.

### Block B1: Main result — certified dominance over exhaustive nominal selection (MUST-RUN)

- **Claim tested:** C1.
- **Why this block exists:** the reviewer's highest-value item.
- **Task:** the full declared grid (1,320 cases).
- **Compared systems:**
  - **Proposed:** robust selection $S_R$ (maximize certified $L$, started from $S_N$ plus 8 random starts).
  - **Strongest baseline:** exhaustive nominal-SLNR optimum $S_N$ in the same family and control.
- **Metrics (decisive first):**
  1. Fraction of cases with $L(S_R) > U(S_N)$ (certified improvement).
  2. Fraction with certified gain above 5%.
  3. Median and interquartile range of the certified gain.
  4. Fraction inconclusive (gain ≤ 0).
  - Secondary: desired gain and leakage at the witnesses for $S_R$ and $S_N$, separately.
- **Setup:** `python idea-stage/pilots/a7_main.py` → `a7_main_results.csv`. Deterministic; about 15–25 CPU-minutes.
- **Success criterion:** a *reported* fraction under both controls, broken down by (ε, SNR); no pass/fail threshold is set after the fact. The mechanism gate used in triage (>5% certified gain in ≥20% of cases) is carried forward as the **pre-declared** go/no-go for keeping C1 as the headline claim (see M1).
- **Failure interpretation (from the reviewer's claims matrix):**
  - Gains vanish with identical endpoints → the benefit depends on aperture freedom. Reframe as such; do not claim an aperture-independent advantage.
  - Brackets overlap → claim only an improved guaranteed lower bound.
- **Table / figure target:**
  - **Fig. 2 (main):** certified gain vs ε for each SNR. Two panels (common vs endpoints), with shaded inconclusive fractions.
  - **Table I:** grid summary (fractions, medians) with desired/leakage columns.
- **Priority:** MUST-RUN.

### Block B2: Baseline families and novelty isolation (MUST-RUN, small)

- **Claim tested:** C1 anti-claims. The gain comes from the *certified objective*, not from generic robustification or from ignoring P.
- **Why this block exists:** a reviewer will ask what a simpler robust heuristic or the graduation-report design does.
- **Task:** one SNR/ε slice with high information — SNR 30 dB, $\epsilon \in \{0.03, 0.05\}\lambda$, 33 geometries, both controls (132 cases).
- **Compared systems (at most 3 baseline families):**
  - **B-NOM:** exhaustive nominal SLNR optimum (the B1 comparator).
  - **B-GR:** graduation-report-style corrected design; the desired-only aligned centered N-block, blind to P (N0 physics).
  - **B-SAMP:** sampled-error robust heuristic; maximize the minimum SLNR over 64 random corners via the same swap search, *uncertified*.
- **All systems are evaluated with the same $L$ and $U$.**
- **Metrics:** $L$, $U$, the certified-dominance fraction of $S_R$ over each baseline, and runtime.
- **Setup:** `a7_baselines.py` (to be written; reuses `a7_pilot` tables).
- **Success criterion:** $S_R$ certifiably dominates B-GR in most cases (expected, since B-GR ignores P). **The informative comparison is vs B-SAMP:** equal or better $L$, and fewer cases where the heuristic's apparent robustness is refuted by a witness.
- **Failure interpretation:** if B-SAMP matches $S_R$ in $L$ at lower cost, then the certificate's value is *verification* rather than *selection*. Reframe C1 as "certified verification of robust selections" (still valid).
- **Table / figure target:** Table II (three rows plus the proposed method).
- **Priority:** MUST-RUN.

### Block B3: Simplicity / deletion check and search adequacy (MUST-RUN, small)

- **Claim tested:** each ingredient is needed, and nothing larger is needed.
- **Why this block exists:** defends parsimony (reviewer rubric) and search status.
- **Task:**
  - (a) 12/4 instances: 33 geometries × $\epsilon \in \{0.03, 0.05\}$ × SNR 30 dB, with an exhaustive certificate optimum over all 495 subsets;
  - (b) the B2 slice for the deletions.
- **Compared variants:**
  1. **Search adequacy:** swap search (restarts 1 / 8 / 32) vs the exhaustive certificate optimum at 12/4. Also exhaustive $\max L$ vs $\max U$ over the family (finite-family verification bound).
  2. **Delete the leakage certificate** (desired-only N1 objective, i.e., "robust desired gain only"): how much certified SLNR is lost?
  3. **Replace the sector bound with a first-order Taylor leakage bound** (Yang-style, no remainder): count violations against sampled witnesses (it is *not* certified) and selection differences.
  4. **Seed sensitivity:** 3 search seeds on the B2 slice.
- **Metrics:** the certificate-optimality gap, the violation count, the certified-SLNR loss, and the seed spread.
- **Setup:** `a7_ablate.py` (to be written).
- **Success criterion:**
  - search reaches ≥ 99% of the exhaustive certificate optimum on ≥ 90% of 12/4 instances;
  - deleting leakage certification loses certified SLNR in a clear majority of cases;
  - the Taylor bound shows violations (justifying the sector enclosure).
- **Failure interpretation:**
  - Search gap is large → report best-found honestly and use 32 restarts.
  - Taylor bound shows no violations on the grid → keep the sector bound for rigor but drop the "needed" claim.
- **Table / figure target:** one compact Table III, or supplementary text.
- **Priority:** MUST-RUN (a, variants 1–2); variants 3–4 NICE-TO-HAVE.

### Block B4: Mechanism and "when" (supporting C2) (MUST-RUN, compact)

- **Claim tested:** C2.
- **Why this block exists:** reviewer requests to explain when robust selection helps, without universal claims.
- **Task:** reuses the B1 CSV; no new runs except the ξ-asymmetry panel.
- **Analyses:**
  1. **Paired mechanism:** for each case, compare $S_R$ vs $S_N$ on (i) nominal leakage $|h_0|^2$, (ii) the first-order directional response (directions mod π), (iii) certified desired gain. Report the fraction of positive cases in which each factor moved favorably. Show one positive and one negative/inconclusive example.
  2. **Noise regime:** apply the fixed-pair condition $(a - c)\sigma^2 > cb - ad$ *pointwise* (selections recomputed at each SNR in B1). Map certified benefit over (ε, SNR).
  3. **Fragility diagnostic:** scatter of certified gain vs $\kappa_b$, colored by ε. Mark the cases where the Lemma 3 remainder is vacuous. No threshold is claimed.
  4. **Bracket tightness:** $U/L$ median, 95th percentile, and maximum by geometry class (co-aligned vs separated receivers).
  5. *(Nice-to-have)* **ξ asymmetry:** fragility of nominal selections vs $x_P$ (feed side vs far side), with the caveat that it concerns ξ, not aggregate ordering.
- **Setup:** `a7_analyze.py` (to be written) reading `a7_main_results.csv`.
- **Success criterion:** a clear, honest decomposition with named counterexamples, and the noise-regime map produced.
- **Failure interpretation:** if no single factor dominates, state that the certificate trades several quantities. This is still publishable.
- **Table / figure target:** **Fig. 3** (mechanism pair + noise regime); a sentence plus the quantiles in the text.
- **Priority:** MUST-RUN (1–4), NICE-TO-HAVE (5).
